Research focus

From developmental signals to meaningful disease models.

My work sits at the intersection of molecular genetics, developmental biology, and precision medicine. I use zebrafish and avian embryo models to examine how genetic and environmental cues influence tissue formation and neurological function.

I bring careful experimental design, practical fluency across the molecular workflow, and a commitment to communicating results clearly - in the lab, at conferences, and with public audiences.

01

Developmental signalling

Mapping the BMP, canonical WNT/β-catenin, and FGF20 pathways that guide avian eye development and bone formation.

02

Precision disease models

Creating and characterizing CRISPR-Cas9 zebrafish models to investigate rare neurological disorders and therapeutic paths.

03

Translational discovery

Connecting expression, phenotype, and imaging data to make rigorous findings useful for diagnosis and future treatment.

Current work · 2024 - present

Investigating how tissue becomes structure.

At Mount Saint Vincent University, I study skeletal and eye development in zebrafish and chicken embryo models. This includes RT-qPCR expression profiling of bone-remodelling genes, HCR RNA FISH, confocal microscopy, and molecular studies of placode formation.
